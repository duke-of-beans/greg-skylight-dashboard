1. Design Vision

The Skylight is a kitchen window into the family's day, not a computer on the wall.
It shows what matters right now, does what you'd otherwise walk across the room for, and disappears when nobody needs it. Greg is behind the glass — present, warm, never pushy.

The market has converged on calendar + lists + photos + routines (Skylight Calendar 2, Hearth, DAKboard, Nest Hub). Every competitor answers two questions: "what are we doing today?" and "what's for dinner?" Our wall answers those too — but it also has Greg: a face, a voice, device control, memory, and the cameras to see who walked in. The differentiator is not features. It's that the wall knows the family and acts on their behalf.

No other product on the market can: speak to a family member by name about their specific day, hold up a paper list and have it land on the shared grocery, tell every TV in the house that dinner is ready, play with a 2-year-old as a responsive voice partner, or remember what happened three years ago today and show the photos. Greg can.

2. Seven Principles

P1 — Customer-obsessed. Rank every feature by steps it removes from the family or moments it brightens. Architectural elegance is not a metric.
P2 — Visibility. Every command to the wall echoes visibly on the wall. "Add milk" opens the list and shows milk appear. "Turn off the TV" shows a confirmation pill. Silent automation is the anti-pattern. The Skylight is a puppet stage; Greg is the puppeteer.
P3 — Ambient by default, utility on demand. 90% of the time the wall is a beautiful photo frame with a thin glance strip. Utility appears when someone walks up, speaks, or taps — and recedes when they leave.
P4 — Organised by moment, personalised on approach. Louvain clustering (Q≈0.09) proves this family's features are shared, not persona-partitioned. The three modes come from arrival context (dinner prep vs after-school vs evening), not from user profiles. Personalization is what the wall leads with when it identifies a face, not which screen it shows.
P5 — One more thing is one too many. Nothing on the wall replaces something the family already does. "Adding another rotating part is the failure mode" (David). The win is a quick action or an unnecessary movement removed from a thing they already do.
P6 — Pre-readers are first-class. Icons beat words. Targets beat menus. Sound beats silence. Lilly (2) and Grammy share this principle from opposite ends — if it works for both, it works.
P7 — Whichever is easier at the time. Wall = capture, consolidate, print. Paper = shop. Phone = shop when paper isn't there, and the catch-up. Voice = the exception report. Every surface handles the same data; the best surface for this moment wins.

4. Proximity Zones

After Vogel & Balakrishnan (UIST 2004). Their four-zone framework for ambient displays maps directly to a kitchen wall.

Ambient
>8 ft
Photos + glance strip. Readable across the room. No interaction possible or expected.
Implicit

Subtle
2–4 ft
Face confirmed. The wall shifts to your personalised lead. Mode tiles fade in. Greg may speak ("Morning — rain by 2, grab an umbrella").
Personal
<2 ft
Touch range. Full interaction available. Cards, lists, calendar, play. The wall is yours.
Implementation

Zone transitions driven by greg-arducam-monitor on Sentinel (/v1/room-presence — face count + size). Face size (bbox area / frame area) maps roughly to distance. Size thresholds calibrated from the actual Arducam's 160° FOV once mounted at final wall height. Face identification from YuNet on Sentinel (greg-face-detect.service) cross-references Throwbak face clusters when built; until then, unknown = ring 4, enrolled = ring by 2FA. Transitions use CSS transition: opacity 300ms ease — never instant.

5. State Machine

Every screen the wall can show is one of six states. Transitions are smooth (200–300ms opacity/transform) and always reversible.

Transition Rules

Resting → Mode: tap a mode tile, voice command ("Greg, what's for dinner"), or auto-select by time-of-day + detected person. Mode → Card: tap an item within the mode. Card → Mode: back gesture, home tile, or 60s idle. Any → Overlay: Greg pushes a display event (generated content, confirmation pill, intercom). Overlays have a countdown timer (4–15s by type) and dismiss on tap. Any → Resting: 60s of no interaction. Resting → Sleep: midnight cron. Sleep → Resting: 5:30am cron. Always available: voice input bypasses navigation entirely — "add milk" works from any state.

6. Resting State

This is the most important screen. It's what the family sees 90% of the time. It must be beautiful enough to be wall art and informative enough to replace a glance at a phone.

Composition

Full-bleed curated photo (from Google Photos shared albums, Throwbak-curated when built). The photo IS the screen — no chrome, no borders. Over the photo, a thin glance strip at the bottom edge, gradient-protected for readability regardless of photo content.

Design rationale

The glance strip is the "10-foot UI" — readable across the kitchen at 6+ feet. Minimum 22pt (Inter 600) for the advice line, 18pt for secondary elements. The gradient overlay is rgba(0,0,0,0.75) fading to transparent over 120px. Photo transitions: 20s crossfade (slower than the current 15s — Odom et al. CHI 2014 "Designing for Slowness" found that slower cadence supports anticipation and re-visitation). The photo never moves or zooms — stillness on a wall is calm (Weiser & Brown, "Calm Technology," 1996).

Presence Reveals

When the Arducam detects a face (implicit zone), the glance strip gently expands upward to include: the next 3 events by person, a "speak to add" affordance (mic icon with gentle pulse), and the mode tiles (4 rounded rectangles at the bottom). This expansion is 300ms ease-out, feels like the wall waking up. On face identification (subtle/personal zone), the events re-sort to lead with that person's items and Greg may greet.

All three use the same family.ts add path — one canon list.

8b. Meals / Tonight

The #1 or #2 question kids ask daily: "what's for dinner?" (Forbes, Cubby, Taste of Home). The wall must answer this before anyone asks.

"Tonight" Card

Auto-surfaces ~4–5pm. Shows tonight's meal with a photo (TheMealDB or generated), ingredients, and a "Cook" button that opens cook mode. If no meal is planned, Greg suggests 2–3 options based on: what's in the pantry (staples model), what the family has eaten recently (don't repeat), dietary constraints (from the family model), and what's quick (weeknight vs weekend). One tap picks a suggestion and sets tonight's meal.

Ingredients → Dish Suggestions (David's directive)

"The family often has ingredients and a vague idea but no recipe." Voice: "Greg, we have chicken thighs and rice" → Greg suggests 3 dishes (Brave search + TheMealDB + LLM) with cook times and difficulty. Tap one to see the recipe. "Add missing ingredients" adds what's needed to the grocery list.

Cook Mode

Full-screen recipe with auto-parsed timers. Step-by-step with large text (24pt minimum — reading from across the kitchen with wet hands). Timer extracted from recipe text ("simmer 20 minutes" → a 20-minute timer on screen). Greg reads the current step aloud on voice command ("Greg, next step"). Timer rings and Greg announces.

Recipe Capture

Grammy's handwritten recipe cards. Hold the card up to the Arducam: "Greg, save this recipe" → vision extract → structured recipe in the recipe collection. Also works by MMS (photo a cookbook page to Greg's number). Same pattern as the grocery camera ingest.

8c. Intercom / "Dinner's Ready"

One utterance reaches the house. The Echo Show's most-used feature is one-way announcements.

The flow

"Greg, dinner's ready" → (1) Pause every playing Roku via ECP Play command on playing devices, (2) Greg speaks it on the Fire speaker + Skylight speaker, (3) Roku TVs: attempt "Play on Roku" channel (ECP launch/2213?u={mp3_url}&t=a) — if the channel isn't available, use a sideloaded Greg dev channel per device that plays audio + shows on-screen text, (4) Fire TV sticks (WG-06): ADB toast. Grammy's .156 constraint is no_audible_keypress (no beep on her TV) — her room still gets the audio announcement through a different path (Sentinel TTS served as MP3).

Ring gate

Intercom is ring ≤ 4 (family). At ring 5 (unknown face present), no broadcast.

9. Kids Mode

Lilly Dwight Greg

The after-school and bedtime mode. This is the wall's differentiator — no competitor has a responsive voice+face partner for a 2-year-old, and no competitor can enforce "finish your routine to unlock the TV."

9a. Routines & Rewards

Hearth's game-changer: picture-step routines that kids operate themselves. Our version adds Greg's voice and device enforcement.

Dwight's Routines (age 7)

Three routines: morning, after-school, bedtime. Each is a vertical list of picture steps (SVG illustration + label, one per line). Walk-up one-tap complete — no login, no parent needed. Confetti on step completion (informational celebration, not points — Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999: tangible contingent rewards undermine intrinsic motivation in children, d = −0.28 to −0.40). Streak tracking ("5 days in a row!") is informational.

Greg-Enforced Unlocks (David's directive)

"Complete X → unlock Y." Routine completion gates the Roku/content — Greg literally puts the show on. This is the feature no competitor can close the loop on. Implementation: routine complete → family_events marks the unlock → content-by-name requests check the unlock flag → Greg: "Nice work — what do you want to watch?" Unlocks attach to CHORES only, never to things Dwight already loves (reading, Legos). The unlock is celebration-adjacent: "you earned this" framing, not "you can't have this until."

Lilly's Bedtime Ritual (age 2)

3 giant icon steps: pajamas, brush teeth, story. Each step is a huge SVG with a single tap to complete. Animation + sound on complete (no confetti — simpler, a warm glow and Greg saying "yay!"). This is a ritual/participation, not a chore — celebration, not scorekeeping.

Today's Feelings (Dwight)

Hearth's novel feature: 16 expressive emoji check-in, logged to a parent Insights page. Ours: Greg asks once ("How are you feeling today, buddy?"), Dwight taps an emotion face, Greg responds warmly and it's logged to family_events with type feeling. Never evaluative, never "why?"

9b. Lilly Play Mode

Research foundation

Roseberry, Hirsh-Pasek & Golinkoff (Child Development 2014, N=36): 24–30-month-olds only learned novel verbs in socially contingent interactions. Troseth/Strouse (Frontiers 2018, N=176): responsiveness alone not sufficient without a co-viewer. Sesame Workshop "Best Practices" (2012): large isolated hot spots, immediate payoff, no text. Toca Boca: no rules, no scores, no failure states. Attention span ~4–6 min per activity at age 2.

Design Rules

No-fail. No-score. Tap-only (no drag — fails under age 5). Ignore accidental/multi-touch. Targets ≥2cm isolated (Sesame Workshop). Immediate audio + visual payoff on every tap. Greg reacts by name with contingent voice ("Good one, Lilly!"). Generous listening — no rushed timeout. Gesture accepted for speech. NEVER "wrong." Rotate 4–6 min activities to match attention span.

Greg as a curiosity engine. The market gives 7-year-olds chores and approved content. Nothing gives them a curious adult who remembers what they're into.

12. Invariants

These apply in every state, every mode, every interaction.

12.1 — Presence Zones

Vogel & Balakrishnan zones (section 4). The wall adapts to distance continuously, not on tap.

12.2 — Face Personalisation

When the wall knows who you are, it leads with YOUR content. Angela sees her events first. Dwight sees his routine status. Lilly triggers play mode if she's alone at the wall. Grammy sees her list and large type.

12.3 — Every Command Echoed

"Add milk" → the list opens, milk appears with a brief highlight animation (green flash, 300ms). "Turn off the TV" → 4-second confirmation pill ("TV off — undo"). "Dwight has a dentist Thursday" → calendar opens, event appears. Nothing happens silently.

12.4 — Undo

Every proactive action and every add has a 10-second undo window. The confirmation pill includes "Undo" as a tap target. Undo reverses the action completely. Horvitz (CHI 1999): minimise the cost of poor guesses.

12.5 — Kid Reach-Height Zone

At the Skylight's mounted height, the bottom third of the screen is within reach of a 2-year-old standing, and the bottom two-thirds for a 7-year-old. In Kids mode, all interactive targets live in the bottom two-thirds. Adult-only controls (settings, admin) live in the top edge, unreachable without standing on something.

12.6 — Display Log

portfolio.greg_display_log records every push: what was shown, who was detected, tapped/dismissed/timed-out, time of day. This replaces the GTIA persona priors with measured data. Re-run gtia.py monthly with real weights.

12.7 — Friction Catalog (WG-36)

Greg catalogs every friction point: unrecognised utterances, abandoned taps, repeated commands, dismissed suggestions, "I wish it could..." All stored as gaps (gaps_form, domain wall). Reviewed in NIGHTSHIFT. The wall grows with the family.

Design Language

Dark-mode-first (a kitchen wall at night must not blind). Sustained-use rules from GRAVITY-00: background saturation <10%, no pure black (#1C1917 not #000000), off-white text (#E7E5E4 not #FFFFFF). Affect Tint (WG-12)

Greg's mood (SNARC phasic relay, already reaching the Skylight via WS) subtly shifts the background warmth. Neutral = the Walnut palette. High valence = warmer undertone. Low valence = cooler undertone. The shift is barely perceptible — a 5–10% hue rotation on the background, nothing more. This is a shared family surface, not a mood ring.

Grammy's Day

Walks into kitchen. Face detected (or unidentified → ring 4). Glance strip with weather (large text), her events. She wants groceries: "Greg, put prune juice on my list" → her list opens, prune juice appears. She taps the Household tile → her list is pre-selected (detected as Grammy). Guest

Unknown face → ring 5. Resting state stays. Glance strip: time, weather, nothing personal. Mode tiles don't appear. The wall is a beautiful photo frame. "Greg, what time is it?" works. Nothing else.

16. Proactive Speech

The wall is Greg's proactive surface (canon). He initiates — not just responds.

Two Sources by Ring

RingAudienceWhat Greg offers
≤ 4Family (default)"Angela, parent conference at 3 and rain at 2 — umbrella." / "Dwight's birthday is in 11 days." / "You're usually due for eggs — want me to add them?"
≤ 2David alone, room emptygaps_queue items, overnight build results, conversation_seeds, portfolio signals, belief challenges.

Rules

One topic per initiation. Offer, don't impose. Read the room first (presence stable for 3+ seconds, no active conversation). Don't repeat declined items for 24h. Ring-gate everything — David's build reports never surface with the family present. The highest-salience READY item for THAT audience wins.

17. Morning Brief

A scheduled state of the resting layer (6–8am school days), not a mode.

Content

Agenda by person (colour-coded). Weather with dress hint per person ("Angela: layers. Dwight: shorts are fine"). Precipitation alert. Angela's commute time. Tonight's dinner preview (if set). "3 things today" summary. Greg speaks it once to whoever walks in first, then the brief stays on screen as a glance card until dismissed or 9am.
