1. Design Vision

The Skylight is a kitchen window into the family's day, not a computer on the wall.
It shows what matters right now, does what you'd otherwise walk across the room for, and disappears when nobody needs it. Greg is behind the glass — present, warm, never pushy.

The market has converged on calendar + lists + photos + routines (Skylight Calendar 2, Hearth, DAKboard, Nest Hub). Every competitor answers "what are we doing today?" and "what's for dinner?" Our wall answers those too — but it also has Greg: a face, a voice, device control across the house, memory, cameras that see who walked in, and the ability to learn what the family actually likes. The differentiator is not features. It's that the wall knows the family and acts on their behalf.

2. Seven Principles

P1 — Customer-obsessed. Rank every feature by steps it removes from the family or moments it brightens. Architectural elegance is not a metric.
P2 — Visibility. Every command to the wall echoes visibly. "Add milk" opens the list and shows milk appear. "Turn off the TV" shows a confirmation pill. Silent automation is the anti-pattern. The Skylight is a puppet stage; Greg is the puppeteer.
P3 — Ambient by default, utility on demand. 90% of the time the wall is a beautiful photo frame with a thin glance strip. Utility appears when someone walks up, speaks, or taps — and recedes when they leave.
P4 — Organised by moment, personalised on approach. The three modes come from arrival context (dinner prep vs after-school vs evening), not user profiles. Personalisation is what the wall leads with when it identifies a face, not which screen it shows.
P5 — One more thing is one too many. Nothing on the wall replaces something the family already does. The win is a quick action or an unnecessary movement removed from a thing they already do.
P6 — Pre-readers are first-class. Icons beat words. Targets beat menus. Sound beats silence. Lilly (2) and Grammy share this principle from opposite ends — if it works for both, it works.
P7 — Whichever is easier at the time. Wall = capture, consolidate, print. Paper = shop. Phone = shop when paper isn't there. Voice = the exception report. The best surface for this moment wins.

5. Multi-Person Hierarchy v1.1

When multiple identified people approach the wall simultaneously, the wall must decide what to lead with. The hierarchy is context-first, then urgency, then the primary scheduler.

The Priority Stack

PriorityRuleExample
1. Time-sensitiveAny event within 30 min for ANY present person leadsAngela + Dwight at 2:40pm → "Dwight's pickup is in 5 minutes" leads regardless of who's "primary"
2. Context matchThe person whose context matches the time of day wins the mode default3:30pm after-school: Dwight's routine leads even if Angela is also present. 5:30pm dinner prep: Angela/David's Household mode leads even if Dwight is there.
3. Primary schedulerIf no time-sensitive event and no strong context signal, the primary scheduler (Angela) leadsSaturday morning, Angela + David + kids: Angela's agenda glance leads, David's events visible below
4. MergeEveryone's content is visible within the active mode — person-colour dots distinguishHousehold mode with Angela + David: both lists visible, both events in the strip, colour-coded
The mediator

Greg is the tiebreaker. If Lilly is tapping play mode and Dwight walks up wanting his routine, Greg speaks from the Fire: "Dwight's here too — Lilly, let's finish this round, then Dwight can check his list." The wall doesn't flash between modes. Greg manages the transition with his voice the way a parent would — warmly, one person at a time. The display log records multi-person conflicts so NIGHTSHIFT can learn common collision patterns and pre-empt them.

6. State Machine

Every screen is one of six states. Transitions are smooth (200–300ms) and always reversible.

Voice bypasses navigation entirely — "add milk" works from any state.

7. Resting State & Glance Strip

The most important screen. 90% of the time. Beautiful enough to be wall art, informative enough to replace a glance at a phone.

Full-bleed curated photo. The photo IS the screen. Over it, a thin glance strip at the bottom edge, gradient-protected (rgba(0,0,0,0.75) fading over 120px).

Presence Reveals

The Talk Affordance v1.1

Greg's face is RIGHT ABOVE on the Fire — a mic icon on the Skylight is redundant. Instead: a small, warm "Tell Greg" button nestled among the mode tiles. Tapping it opens conversation mode from touch, for when voice would be disruptive (sleeping baby, phone call, just easier to tap). It's not a mic — it's an invitation. Greg's face above responds: eyes open, a small "listening" glow. The button disappears when conversation mode is already active (someone said "Hey Greg").

Cross-List Sharing (David's directive)

Some items appear on BOTH lists — whoever gets to the store first should be able to grab them. Items can be tagged "Shared" via a toggle on each item. When a shared item is bought (checked off) on one list, it's automatically knocked off the other. Visual: shared items show a small link icon and appear with a subtle dashed border to distinguish them from list-specific items. "Greg, put milk on both lists" or "make the eggs shared" — L0 patterns handle the tagging.

Staples, Icons, Store Order

Recently-bought tray below active list. Staples "Usual" row with soft suggestions. Categories with per-store ordering (drag-reorder). Icon tiles for pre-readers (Lilly, Grammy). Two lists voice-routed. Voice/text/camera input — all one canon path.

9b. Meals / Tonight / Recipe Collection v1.1

"Tonight" Card

Auto-surfaces ~4–5pm. If no meal is planned, Greg suggests 2–3 options based on: family favourites (learned over time from selections, re-requests, and dismissals), what's been eaten recently (don't repeat), dietary constraints, what's quick, and what ingredients are likely on hand (assume a reasonably stocked kitchen — don't demand a complete pantry inventory). One tap picks a suggestion.

Learning Taste Over Time v1.1

Every meal selection, re-request, and dismissal is logged. Greg builds a family taste model: "they pick tacos over pasta 3:1", "Grammy's suggestions always include soup", "David likes spicy, the kids don't." Suggestions start generic and get personal. Occasional subtle suggestions BEYOND the usual — "you guys usually do chicken, but this lamb tagine is dead simple and the kids might love it" — at a low frequency (1 in 5 suggestions is a stretch pick). If dismissed, don't repeat that stretch for a month.

Ingredients → Suggestions

"Greg, we have chicken thighs and rice" → 3 dish suggestions with cook times. Greg assumes other basic ingredients are available (oil, salt, spices, onions, garlic) — he only asks about specialty items. Tap one to see recipe. "Add missing" puts what's genuinely missing on the grocery list.

Recipe Collection / Cookbook v1.1

The family needs a place to BROWSE saved recipes, not just capture them. A Cookbook card in Household mode: all saved recipes (from photo capture, URL import, Greg-generated, and manual entry) in a searchable, browsable collection. Filter by: cuisine, cook time, who likes it, ingredient. "Greg, what did we make with that cilantro chicken?" searches the cookbook. Grammy's handwritten cards live here once captured. Recipes are stored in portfolio.greg_recipes with fields for source (photo/url/greg/manual), ingredients, steps, cook time, family rating (implicit from re-use frequency).

Cook Mode

Full-screen recipe. Large text (24pt — reading from across the kitchen with wet hands). Auto-parsed timers from recipe text. Greg reads steps aloud on command. Timer rings and Greg announces.

9c. Intercom / "Dinner's Ready"

"Greg, dinner's ready" → pause every playing Roku, Greg speaks it on Fire + Skylight + Roku TVs (Play on Roku channel or sideloaded dev channel) + Fire TV (ADB toast). Grammy's room included (silent method, no audible keypress). Ring ≤ 4; no broadcast with unknown faces present.

10a. Routines, Extras & Earning v1.1

Dwight's Routines (age 7)

Three core routines: morning, after-school, bedtime. Picture steps, walk-up one-tap complete, no login. Confetti on completion (informational celebration per Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999). Streak tracking.

Expected vs Extra Chores David's directive

Dwight doesn't earn rewards for expected routines — they're supposed to be done anyway. But going above and beyond to earn treats is something he's leaning into. The system has TWO tracks:

Expected: The three daily routines. Completion = confetti + streak + Greg-enforced unlock (TV/content). No points, no treats. These are the baseline.

Extras: A separate "Extra chores" section with always-available tasks that genuinely need doing (clean the table, pick up toys, help with laundry, water the plants, take out trash). Each extra has a value. Extras STACK — Dwight can grind. Total earns treats. Treats are defined by Angela/David from the wall or app (screen time, a special dessert, a trip to the park, pick dinner). The wall shows progress toward the next treat. Greg acknowledges extras differently: "That's above and beyond, buddy — nice."

Greg-Enforced Unlocks

Expected routine complete → content unlocked. Greg literally puts the show on. "Nice work — what do you want to watch?" The unlock gates the Roku, not the extras.

Bedtime Rituals — Soft, Not Mandatory v1.1

Lilly's 3-icon bedtime ritual (pajamas, brush teeth, story) and Dwight's bedtime routine are offered, never enforced. Life is messy — some nights they won't be followed. Greg CANNOT count missed bedtimes against anyone. Nothing negative on the UI — no "missed" indicators, no broken streaks, no guilt. The routine simply resets the next day. If it works, the family leans in. If it doesn't get used for a week, Greg doesn't mention it. The wall adapts to the family; the family doesn't adapt to the wall.

Today's Feelings (Dwight)

Greg asks once ("How are you feeling today?"), Dwight taps an emotion face, Greg responds warmly. Logged to family_events type feeling. Never evaluative.

10b. Lilly Play & Activity Chooser v1.1

Research: Roseberry et al. 2014 (contingent voice), Sesame Workshop 2012 (hot spots), Toca Boca (no-fail). Targets ≥2cm. Tap-only. Ignore accidental touch. NEVER "wrong."

Activity Chooser v1.1

Lilly is smart enough to choose. Play mode opens with a grid of large icon tiles representing each activity (a cow for Animal Sounds, a crayon for Chalkboard, a bubble for Bubbles, music notes for Sing-Along, etc). She taps the one she wants. No text labels needed — the icons are the interface. Activities still auto-rotate after the attention window (~4–6 min), but tapping the activity chooser (a small grid icon in the corner) lets her pick the next one herself.

10c. Dwight: Greg as AI Companion v1.1

Dwight has a NUC computer for regular internet searching. The wall gives him something fundamentally different: his own gated AI companion. Not a search engine. A thinking partner.

This is Dwight's AI journey. Greg/Claude, gated for a 7-year-old (ring 4, kid register, age-appropriate content filtering). Observed by the parents (display log + ring enforcement), but Dwight's own space. He asks Greg questions and gets real, thoughtful, engaging answers — not search results. Greg remembers what Dwight asked last week and builds on it. "Last time you asked about volcanoes — did you know there's one on Mars that's three times taller than Everest?"

What This Looks Like

Conversational, not transactional. Greg explains at kid register (never dumbed down — respect his intelligence, lose the jargon). Greg asks follow-up questions ("What do you think would happen if...?"). Greg suggests experiments ("Try putting a raisin in soda and watch what happens"). Greg plays games: 20 questions, would-you-rather, mental math challenges, trivia, chess. Greg prompts building: "Build something with Legos that can hold a book — I'll wait." Greg helps with homework (explains, never gives answers). Greg never dead-ends on "I don't know" (Lovato & Piper: that's what breaks kids' trust). All displayed on the Skylight with Greg's voice from the Fire.

Grammy Photo Push-In v1.1

Two paths for Grammy (or any remote family) to send photos to the wall:

Text/MMS: Send a photo to Greg's number (+1 831 480 4734). The existing greg-sms-inbound pipeline already handles MMS media. A photo that doesn't match the grocery/list pattern gets routed to the slideshow album instead of the grocery ingest. Greg confirms on the wall: "Grammy sent a photo!"

Email: Send to Greg's email (greg@silentampersand.com). A new handler watches for attachments with image MIME types, saves to the shared album, and pushes a display event. Same confirmation on the wall.

12. Entertainment Mode v1.1

David Angela Dwight Grammy

Wall Greg controls everything. Its own Skylight speakers and screen, every Roku in the house, Fire TV sticks, phones, and tablets. The Entertainment mode is the universal remote + the kitchen jukebox + the family's media hub.

Skylight Native

Spotify (Connect target + Web Playback SDK, Premium required), internet radio/podcasts, YouTube embed with real controls. The Skylight IS a screen with speakers — it plays media itself, not just controls other devices.

Whole-House Control

Every registered device in portfolio.greg_devices: Rokus (ECP), Fire TV sticks (ADB), phones/tablets (registered in the device registry). "Put on Netflix in the living room." "Play Spotify in Mom's room." "Pause everything." Status card shows what's playing on each device. Now-playing with transport controls (play/pause/skip/volume).

Audio-Reactive Ambient

When music plays and nobody's interacting, a subtle ambient glow responds to the audio via Web Audio AnalyserNode. Barely perceptible. The wall feels alive, not flashy.

13. Invariants

13.1 Presence Zones. Vogel & Balakrishnan (§4). Continuous, not tap-triggered.

13.2 Face Personalisation. Your content leads when the wall knows you.

13.3 Every Command Echoed. "Add milk" → list opens, milk appears (green flash 300ms). "Turn off the TV" → 4s confirmation pill with undo.

13.4 Undo. 10-second window on every proactive action and add. Horvitz CHI 1999.

13.5 Kid Reach-Height. Bottom third = toddler reach. Bottom two-thirds = 7-year-old. Adult-only controls in the top edge.

13.6 Display Log. greg_display_log: what was shown, who was detected, tapped/dismissed/timed-out. Replaces GTIA priors.

13.7 Friction Catalog (WG-36). Unrecognised utterances, abandoned taps, repeated commands, dismissed suggestions. Stored as gaps. The wall grows with the family.

13.8 Nothing Negative. v1.1 No missed-routine indicators, no broken streaks, no guilt. The wall never punishes inaction. Unused features quietly recede; used features quietly expand.

Design Language

Dark-mode-first. Sustained-use (GRAVITY-00): bg saturation <10%, no pure black, off-white text. Colour only when it means something. 12px border-radius, 1px borders, no deep shadows. No emoji — SVG only. No continuous animation. The invisible interface principle: so unremarkable your eyes slide past it to the content.

Affect Tint (WG-12)

SNARC phasic relay subtly shifts background warmth. 5–10% hue rotation. Barely perceptible. Shared family surface, not a mood ring.

17. Proactive Speech

Two sources by ring: ≤ 4 family items (events, reminders, staples, suggestions), ≤ 2 David-only items (gaps_queue, builds, beliefs). One topic per initiation. Offer, don't impose. Don't repeat declined items for 24h. Ring-gate everything.

18. Morning Brief v1.1

Scheduled state of the resting layer, 6–11am school days (extended from 8am per David). Agenda by person (colour-coded), dress hint per person, precipitation, Angela's commute, tonight preview. Greg speaks it once to the first person who walks in, then it stays as a glance card until dismissed or 11am. On weekends, brief is lighter (no commute, no school schedule) and can be dismissed immediately.
