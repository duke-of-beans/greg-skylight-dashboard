## TL;DR
- Across every function, the market has converged on the same spine — glanceable-at-idle, tap-to-act, voice-to-add, photos-when-nobody's-looking — but the leaders (Skylight Calendar 2, Hearth, Aura, Nest Hub) win on cutting steps: AI paper-to-calendar import, one-tap chore completion with no login, on-device photo curation, and one-way "dinner's ready" intercom. Wall Greg can beat all of them by combining these with a responsive voice+face the commercial products lack.
- The single best-evidenced design lever for the toddler is social contingency: a responsive voice/face partner is developmentally superior to passive video (Roseberry, Hirsh-Pasek & Golinkoff, Child Development 85(3):956–970, 2014; N=36, 24–30 months), delivered through no-fail, no-score, tap-only, big-target, immediate-audio-payoff play (Toca Boca/Sago Mini; Sesame Workshop 2012) — but for a 2-year-old paired with joint media engagement, not left as a babysitter.

## Function 1 — WEATHER
Best-in-class. Apple Weather (WeatherKit) and CARROT both lead on next-hour, minute-by-minute precipitation and animated radar. CARROT's mechanic is a configurable personality writing a one-line summary over the data, plus government severe alerts and morning/evening reports. "What to wear" is exemplified by Weather Fit: a customizable avatar dressed for today's forecast with a "comfort slider" (runs hot/cold), animated landscape scenes (City/Mountains/Beach/Village) whose sky shifts with time of day, and next-hour precipitation. Windy leads on layered maps; Weather Underground on hyperlocal personal-weather-station data.
Praise/pain. CARROT ~4.7 stars; praise for personality and Watch complications; complaints center on an aggressive multi-tier paywall (radar and complications moved behind Premium after being paid-for), rough onboarding, and the personality wearing thin. Widget reviews prize hyperlocal + minute-by-minute precipitation and one-glance clarity.
Research. Pousman & Stasko's ambient-information taxonomy (AVI 2006): Information Capacity, Notification Level, Representational Fidelity, Aesthetic Emphasis. Mankoff, Dey et al., "Heuristic evaluation of ambient displays" (CHI 2003); Matthews et al. peripheral-display toolkit (UIST 2004); Weiser & Brown "Calm Technology" (1996).
Delight. Weather-as-scene art; a dressed avatar per family member; "good day for the park" lines; cartoon-sun/rain animation with animal reactions for kids.
Design implications. An idle-glance card at low information capacity (icon + temp + one advice line: "Umbrella today — rain by 3pm"), readable across the kitchen. On Open-Meteo, Greg generates the advice line and per-persona dress hints. Render a scene, not a table. Avoid CARROT's paywall/onboarding mistakes. For Lilly, a tappable weather scene with animal sounds is a toddler activity, not information.

## Function 2 — GROCERY / SHOPPING LISTS
Best-in-class. AnyList — recipe-driven: imports recipes from any page, plans meals, generates a combined list that merges duplicates; automatic aisle sorting, multiple stores; voice add via Siri/Alexa/Google; ~$12–15/yr premium. OurGroceries — fastest real-time sync; custom aisle categories per store; barcode scan to re-add empties; recipe→list; voice add; free with ads; persistent complaint: no price tracking; dated UI. Bring! — best for pre-readers/non-tech: visual icon tiles remove reading/typing; one-tap suggestions. Listonic — fast setup, aisle sorting, running total. Out of Milk — item-level shared updates. Google Keep/Apple Reminders — zero-setup, voice/image capture; Reminders auto-categorizes into aisles. Grocy — open-source "household ERP": stock/inventory with quantities and expiry, consume/purchase workflow, list generation from stock minimums — the gold standard for the item lifecycle model. Skylight/Hearth lists — on-wall tap-to-check; Skylight can transfer lists to AnyList; Sidekick auto-writes the list from a meal plan.
Praise/pain. #1 requirement is reliability — "when your partner adds 'diapers' at 3pm it will be there at 5pm." Praise: sync, aisle sorting, voice add. Pain: aisle setup gets skipped; no price tracking; crippled free tiers; privacy concerns. Bring!'s icons are the clear win for households with children or non-tech members.
Research. Household provisioning is core CSCW; Sellen, Hyams & Eardley "The Everyday Problems of Working Parents" (HP Labs 2004) and Beech et al. "The Lifestyles of Working Parents" (HP Labs 2004) document provisioning as mental-load work carried disproportionately by one member.
Delight. Icon lists a toddler or Grammy can read; "recently bought" quick re-add; auto-surfacing staples; barcode/camera add of an empty carton; per-person lists that merge for the shopper.
Design implications. The current card's named defects — tap-to-check has no clear meaning, no delete, no categories — are fixed with an explicit lifecycle: needed → in-cart (struck through) → bought (drops to a "recently bought, tap to re-add" tray) → removable. Add store/aisle categories and history autocomplete. Keep Grammy's list visually separate but voice-routable. Add icon tiles so Lilly and Dwight can add by picture. Every voice add echoes on the wall. Consider one-way handoff to AnyList.

## Function 3 — FAMILY CALENDAR
Best-in-class. Skylight Calendar 2 — syncs Google/Outlook/Apple/Cozi/Yahoo (full two-way only with Google); color-coded profiles, day/week/month/agenda + per-person filter; Magic Import / Sidekick: forward a school email, PDF, flyer photo, or handwritten note → AI creates events and can assign one event to multiple profiles; syncs TeamSnap; Plus $79/yr. Hearth — 4.7/5 (786 reviews); all views; companion app is where most managing happens; native parenting-pro routines; 113 features in two years. Cozi — decade-old default (20M+ users); color per member, shared lists, meal planner, recipe box; Cozi Gold $39/yr; Cozi Max $79/yr adds AI Event Import/Recipe Creator/Meal Planner; free tier capped at a 30-day window — the 2024 cap drew Trustpilot reviews averaging ~2.1 stars ("bait and switch"): contribution/access must stay frictionless or the artifact goes unused. TimeTree — "loved by 75 million users worldwide" (vendor); unlimited shared calendars; per-event Memo/chat thread; ~4.6 stars across ~200,000 reviews. Google/Apple Family — free, reliable, no built-in per-person color.
Praise/pain. "Shared calendars don't redistribute the mental load, they just display it more efficiently." Praise: Magic Import; two-way Google sync; color coding. Pain: Wi-Fi-dependent; best features paywalled; only Google gets full two-way sync; Cozi's dated UI and 30-day cap.
Research. Neustaedter, Brush & Greenberg, "The Calendar is Crucial," ACM TOCHI 16(1), Art. 6 (2009), 48 pp. — 44 families; calendars maintained overwhelmingly by one person (Brush & Turner 2005: 72% single maintainer; other data ~90% women); family types monocentric / pericentric / polycentric. LINC inkable calendar (Neustaedter & Brush, CHI 2006; Graphics Interface 2007) — families need inkability/annotation, kitchen placement, low-friction batch entry (schools email date lists that "though digital… could not be readily incorporated"). Plaisant et al. "Shared Family Calendars," TOCHI 13(3) (2006); Palen (CHI 1999); Payne "Understanding Calendar Use," HCI 8(2) (1993). "FamilyCanvas: Designing a Digital Calendar for Blended Families," CHI 2026 Extended Abstracts; Odom, Zimmerman & Forlizzi, DIS 2010.
Delight. Countdowns ("3 sleeps until Grandma"); conflict detection; per-event memo; one event to several people; a "primary-scheduler relief" mode where Greg drafts the week and Angela approves.
Design implications. Angela is the likely monocentric maintainer — the north star is reducing her entry burden, not adding views. Greg's Magic-Import equivalent (school flyer photo → events) is the single highest-value feature. Default to today's agenda + this-week glance, month grid on tap. Color-per-person stays. Two-way write-back. Conflict/countdown surfacing is low-cost delight.

## Function 4 — MEAL PLANNING / "WHAT'S FOR DINNER"
Best-in-class. Skylight Sidekick — from a natural-language prompt ("one diabetic person, focus on protein, we have ground beef and chicken") it generated a 7-day dinner plan with recipes and auto-added ingredients to the list; recipe capture from a photo of a cookbook page or handwritten card, and from URLs; shows tonight's meal. Hearth — Recipe Vault; AI meal planning. Paprika — clip from any site, auto-detect cook timers in recipe text, pantry tracking with expiry, reusable menus, scaling, grocery list that consolidates shared ingredients; one-time purchase. Mealime, Plan to Eat (~50% less planning time), Eat This Much, Cozi meals, Whisk/Samsung Food.
Praise/pain. Job-to-be-done: ending "the 'what's for dinner?' stress." Skylight's list output is criticized for dumping ingredients only into Skylight's own list. Paprika's UI is utilitarian. Manual planners "require consistent effort to stay useful."
Research. Family meal decision-making sits in the working-parents literature (Sellen et al. 2004; Beech et al. 2004); "what's for dinner" is a recurring mental-load task on the primary manager.
Delight. A wall that answers "what's for dinner" before anyone asks (proactive tonight-card ~4–5pm); recipe-from-photo; auto list from plan; "use what we have" ingredient search; kid-facing "Taco Tuesday" icon.
Design implications. Highest-value/lowest-effort: a "Tonight" answer at dinner-prep time, generated by Greg, with tap-through recipe and one-tap "add missing ingredients." Photo-capture suits Grammy. Generate lists into their list with lifecycle semantics, not a silo.

## Function 5 — KIDS' ROUTINES, CHORES, REWARDS, EMOTIONAL CHECK-INS
Best-in-class. Hearth Routines & Stars — picture icons per step; parenting-coach native routines; per-step star values; streak icon with day count; confetti on step and routine completion; Today's Feelings emoji check-in logged to a parent Insights page; Child Lock; pin one reward to track progress. Skylight Chores — picture-based chores a non-reader checks by image; stars→rewards; Disney characters; parental lock. DAKboard chores — per-person columns, one tap to complete with no app and no login, points→rewards, Child Lock. Greenlight/BusyKid — chore-to-real-allowance; OurHome/Homey — points-based.
Praise/pain. Parents love walk-up-and-tap + confetti and app management. Friction: stars/rewards can only be created/edited from the app, not the wall (Hearth) — a step the wall could remove.
Research (reward debate). SDT (Deci & Ryan) and the overjustification effect (Lepper, Greene & Nisbett 1973; Deci 1971) warn tangible, controlling rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation for already-interesting tasks. Deci, Koestner & Ryan's meta-analysis of 128 studies (Psychological Bulletin 125(6):627–668, 1999): engagement-, completion-, and performance-contingent rewards significantly undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation (d = −0.40, −0.36, −0.28), while positive feedback enhanced free-choice behavior (d = 0.33) and self-reported interest (d = 0.31); "tangible rewards tended to be more detrimental for children than college students." Rewards experienced as informational (competence feedback) help, controlling tangible ones hurt; relatedness + rationale + autonomy support drive internalization (Deci, Eghrari, Patrick & Leone 1994; Ryan & Deci 2000). For chores (not inherently interesting), extrinsic scaffolding is defensible but should tilt informational. Cameron & Pierce dispute the effect's scope.
7 vs 2. A 7-year-old owns a real routine with stars/streaks/pinned goal. A 2-year-old cannot do chores; Lilly's "routine" is a ritual/participation ("put toys in the bin" animation + applause) — celebration, not scorekeeping.
Design implications. Let routines be created and rewarded from the wall, not just an app. Picture steps, big walk-up target at kid reach-height. Prefer informational celebration (streaks, "you did it!", a photo of the finished task) over heavy point economies; expose an autonomy-supporting rationale. Add Today's Feelings for Dwight (optionally adults). Child-lock so Lilly can't clear Dwight's list.

## Function 6 — TODDLER (age 2) ENGAGEMENT WITH A VOICE COMPANION
Best-in-class + principles. Toca Boca and Sago Mini are the reference digital-toy designers: "no rules, scores, or time limits," "no narrator explaining the 'right' way to play"; Sago Mini: open-ended, goal-free play ("no wrong answers, no failure states"; "no instructions because a three-year-old doesn't need them — they just poke things to see what happens"), COPPA/kidSAFE-certified, no ads/IAP. Also Duck Duck Moose (Peekaboo Barn), Khan Academy Kids, Endless Alphabet, Pok Pok, Baby's Musical Hands.
Toddler-UI rulebook. Large, isolated tap targets — NN/g recommends ≥2cm × 2cm for young children (4× adult minimum); Sesame Workshop "Hot Spots" must be "large and adequately isolated." Immediate audio+visual payoff on every action (Sesame Workshop, "Best Practices: Designing Touch Tablet Experiences for Preschoolers," 2012). Tap-only; avoid drag-and-drop under 5 (children "accidentally stop making contact during the drag, losing progress"). No menus / no reliance on text or abstract icons (Hiniker et al., "Hidden Symbols," IJHCS 90, 2016). Forgiving recognition; ignore accidental touches; repeat-until-success; auto-advance if stuck. Touch develops early but imperfectly — simple gestures mastered as early as age 2 (Aziz et al. 2013; Hourcade et al. 2015), 3-year-olds outperform 2-year-olds on all measures (Frontiers 2021 "Toddlers Using Tablets"), adults ~30% more successful than 3–6-year-olds (Vatavu et al. 2015). Hourcade, "Interaction Design and Children" (Found. Trends HCI 2008).
Attention span. Clinical rule of thumb ~2–3 minutes of sustained attention per year of age → a 2-year-old ≈ 4–6 min per activity: rotate short high-interest activities.
Voice-companion research. Social contingency: Roseberry, Hirsh-Pasek & Golinkoff, "Skype Me!" Child Development 85(3):956–970 (2014) — 24–30-month-olds "only learned novel verbs in socially contingent interactions (live interactions and video chat)." The video deficit (Troseth et al. 2006). Caveat: Troseth, Strouse et al., "Let's Chat: On-Screen Social Responsiveness Is Not Sufficient…," Frontiers in Psychology (2018, N=176) — responsiveness alone not sufficient without a co-viewer; joint media engagement still matters (Takeuchi & Stevens 2011; Strouse, O'Doherty & Troseth 2013). Children anthropomorphize agents (Druga et al., "Hey Google is it OK if I eat you?", IDC 2017); younger children form parasocial attachments (Garg & Sengupta, IMWUT 2020; Hoffman & Calvert 2021).
Social robots — loved, then failed. Moxie (Embodied; $799; ages 5–10): missions, jokes, open-ended chat, emotion detection; shutdown notice Nov 26, 2024; Axios (Dec 10, 2024): no refunds; robots bricked because "all of those abilities required a constant connection to a cloud-based AI system." Jibo (2014): expressive, eye contact; failed on thin margins vs cheap Alexa/Google. Lesson: kids form deep attachments to an expressive face+voice — an asset for Wall Greg — but the killer was cloud cost + hardware margin + fuzzy value. Wall Greg avoids all three.
Design implications. For Lilly, a no-fail digital toy with a responsive voice/face: giant tap targets, animation+sound on every touch, no menus/text, tap-only, forgiving of mis-taps, no scores, no dead ends. Rotate short cause-and-effect activities. Greg's contingency — reacting by name, turn-taking, generous listening with no rushed timeouts and multimodal (gesture) acceptance — is what distinguishes this from passive video. Design joint moments ("show Daddy!") not pure solo babysitting. Expect ASR to fail on a 2-year-old's speech: never tell her she's "wrong," offer picture-choice fallbacks, let taps substitute for words.

## Function 7 — 7-YEAR-OLD ENGAGEMENT
Best-in-class. Amazon Kids+ / Echo Show kids' mode: curated Q&A, jokes, "tell me a fun fact," character voices, kid-safe skills, timers, parental controls. Khan Academy Kids. Miko robot.
Research. Lovato & Piper, "'Siri, Is This You?'" (IDC 2015); Lovato, Piper & Wartella, "'Hey Google, Do Unicorns Exist?'" (IDC 2019, 18 families, ages 5–6, two-week in-home): children mostly ask information-seeking questions — science/tech ~24%, culture ~21%, practical ~18%. Key frustration: agents answer "I don't know" to fantasy questions, eroding trust (MIT Tech Review 2019). Oranç & Ruggeri, "'Alexa, let me ask you something different'" (Human Behavior & Emerging Technologies 2021, ages 3–10): older children adapt questions. Danovitch on selective trust: children >5 may initially trust a voice assistant more than a human. Xu & Warschauer: dialogic Q&A aids comprehension.
Design implications. For Dwight, Greg is a curiosity engine: kid-register "why" answers, jokes, science facts, quizzes, "would you rather," 20-questions, mental-math, chess/checkers, Lego-build prompts, reading practice — and never dead-end on "I don't know." Support video calls to grandparents.

## Function 8 — PHOTOS / AMBIENT MODE
Best-in-class. Aura (6M+ app users): unlimited free cloud storage; push-in network (invited family adds from their phones); Aspen (2025, $229) adds on-frame captions and app-side People Search running face recognition locally on-device; motion sensor + auto-brightness. Nest Hub Photo Frame: auto-curates Google Photos (faces, hides blurry/duplicates), "on this day," Ambient EQ. Nixplay: cloud integrations, email-to-frame, playlists; retroactively capped once-unlimited storage (trust-eroding). Frameo: free forever, works offline. Skylight Frame: video/captions/reactions require Plus. Samsung The Frame Art Mode; DAKboard/MagicMirror overlay calendar/weather on photos.
Praise/pain. Aura praised for photo quality, décor integration, auto-brightness, app simplicity; the deciding factor is the sending experience and whether contributing is free — subscription walls make frames "sit unplugged after month two."
Research. Odom, Sellen, Banks, Kirk, Regan, Selby, Forlizzi & Zimmerman, "Designing for Slowness, Anticipation and Re-visitation: A Long-Term Field Study of the Photobox," CHI 2014 (Best Paper) — 14 months in 3 homes; slowness supporting anticipation and re-visitation. Hallnäs & Redström, "Slow Technology" (2001). Durrant et al. on family photo display; Kirk & Sellen on home curation.
Delight. Remote grandparent push-in; kids' artwork gallery; on-frame captions; "on this day"; face-prioritized curation; ambient color/brightness matching; auto-flip from photos to utility on approach.
Design implications. Make the idle slideshow feel like a print: slower cadence, curated, match room brightness (use the camera to dim at night, brighten on approach). Add a push-in path and a kids' artwork gallery. On-photo captions warm Grammy. The camera lets the wall flip from photos to the right person's utility as they walk up.

## Function 9 — MEDIA / TV CONTROL & INTERCOM
Best-in-class. Alexa Announcements / Drop In: "announce dinner is ready" plays a chime then your recorded voice on every Echo; Drop In opens a two-way line; works across Echo, Fire tablets, Fire TV. Google Nest Broadcast: Assistant's voice on all Nest speakers/displays; Family Broadcast reaches family phones and they can reply; no true Drop-In. TV: Roku/Fire TV/Google TV voice control; "put on Paw Patrol" by name; Sonos/Spotify Connect jukebox. DIY: Home Assistant TTS broadcast; MagicMirror alerts.
Praise/pain. The killer everyday use is literally "call the kids for dinner" without shouting. Drop In raises privacy questions.
Design implications. Extend to a one-way "dinner's ready" broadcast to TVs/speakers and Grammy's room. Support content-by-name. Every such command shows on the wall. A two-way "check on the kitchen" must be presence/consent-visible.

## Function 10 — THE AI / ASSISTANT LAYER
Best-in-class. Skylight Sidekick: import events from emails/photos/PDFs; plan meals by dietary need; write the grocery list; an activity planner searching for local events given constraints (area, day/time, who's coming, budget, "vibe"). Hearth Helper + Family Rhythm Summary. Cozi Max AI. Alexa+ / Gemini on Nest (2025–2026 rollouts — verify at build).
Praise/pain. Paper-to-calendar and meal-plan-to-list are the most-praised "wow" features because they cut steps. Friction is siloing. Proactive suggestions delight when relevant, annoy when mistimed.
Research. Horvitz, "Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces" (CHI 1999) — consider uncertainty about goals, consider the user's attention in timing services, minimize the cost of poor guesses, provide easy correction/undo. Horvitz, Koch & Apacible "BusyBody" (CSCW 2004); Iqbal & Horvitz (CSCW 2010); Iqbal & Bailey (CHI 2008); Bailey et al. on interruption cost. Proactive-agent principles: valuable, pertinent, competent, unobtrusive, transparent, controllable, deferent, anticipatory, safe.
Design implications. Greg's suggestion line is a mixed-initiative surface — deferent and correctable: suggest at low-cost moments (idle, morning), never interrupt an active task, every suggestion dismissible and every proactive action undoable, surface on the wall rather than aloud unless addressed. Route AI outputs into the real lists/calendar.

## Function 11 — INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE for wall displays
How the market organizes. Skylight and Hearth default to a calendar-forward hub with bottom/side nav + idle photo mode. Echo Show 15/21: widget wall. Nest Hub Max: ambient photo by default, voice/touch summons cards. DAKboard/MagicMirror: custom blocks. Home Assistant wall panels: entity controls beside calendar/chores/photos, "no app to install and no login." Samsung Family Hub. Common split: ambient (idle) vs summoned (touch/voice).
Research. Pousman & Stasko's four dimensions size the ambient layer. Vogel & Balakrishnan, "Interactive Public Ambient Displays" (UIST 2004): four interaction zones — ambient display → implicit interaction → subtle interaction → personal interaction — as a user moves from far to up-close: the exact model for a kitchen wall. Elliot, Neustaedter & Greenberg, "Time, Ownership and Awareness" (UbiComp 2005) and "Interpersonal Awareness in the Domestic Realm" (OzCHI 2006): the kitchen as household "command center."
Design implications. Adopt the zones explicitly: (1) ambient/far — photos + clock + weather glyph + next event; (2) implicit — as presence is detected, surface the now-relevant card; (3) subtle/personal — on approach/face-ID, show that person's content. Large touch targets at kid reach-height. Minimize chrome — the 8-icon nav is a lot for a glanceable wall; collapse toward ambient + summon.

## Function 12 — WOW / UNIQUE / OUT-OF-THE-BOX
Paper-to-digital via camera (Magic Import). Family "mailbox" / video message board & kid-recorded voice notes that play when the recipient walks up. Countdown & celebration animations. Morning "good morning" briefing with a face. Kids' artwork gallery. Weather-as-scene art and seasonal ambient themes. House "mood" via presence. Guest mode. Grandparent-first simplicity & distance legibility. On-device face recognition to prioritize the right person's content.

## Cross-Cutting (i): Patterns the whole market agrees on
1 Ambient by default, utility on demand. 2 Voice-to-add, tap-to-complete. 3 AI to cut entry steps (paper/photo/PDF/email → events, recipes, lists). 4 Color-per-person + per-person filter. 5 Picture/icon affordances for pre-readers. 6 One-way household broadcast is the most-used intercom behavior. 7 Contribution must be free/frictionless or the artifact goes unused. 8 Reliability and low friction beat features. 9 Mental load concentrates on one maintainer — tools that only display it don't redistribute it. 10 Rewards/celebration for kids are universal — lean informational, not controlling.

## Recommendations (staged, with thresholds)
Stage 1 — Fix broken primitives: grocery item lifecycle with categories and history; every voice command echoes visibly and two-way writes; implement the Vogel & Balakrishnan zone model; collapse the 8-icon nav toward ambient+summon.
Stage 2 — High-wow, step-cutting: photo/flyer → calendar events via the camera; "Tonight's dinner" card + cook mode + "add missing to list"; one-way "dinner's ready" broadcast; print-like curated slideshow + brightness matching + push-in + kids' artwork gallery.
Stage 3 — Toddler + child modes (the differentiator): Lilly's no-fail digital-toy mode with contingent voice and joint moments; Dwight's curiosity-engine mode that never dead-ends; Today's Feelings; picture-step routines with informational celebration.
Stage 4 — Tune the AI layer: deferent, correctable, ambiently-surfaced proactivity.
Thresholds: if the household doesn't trust the shared list/calendar within ~2 weeks, stop adding features and fix reliability. If Lilly's sessions stay under the ~4–6-min per-activity span and she disengages rather than rotating, add variety and stronger contingency. If Greg's suggestions are dismissed >~50% of the time, cut proactivity frequency. If points visibly become the only reason Dwight does a task, shift to informational celebration.

## Caveats
Alexa+/Gemini family capabilities are 2025–2026 rollouts. Review aggregators are secondary and sometimes SEO-driven. Attention-span numbers are a clinical rule of thumb. Contingency evidence is nuanced (Roseberry 2014 vs Troseth/Strouse 2018). Reward research is contested (Cameron & Pierce). Camera presence/face-ID, Drop-In-style check-ins, and emotional check-in logging are sensitive; on-device processing and visible-on-wall consent are the mitigations.
